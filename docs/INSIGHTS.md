# Key Findings (Lok Sabha, 543 PCs, 83.41 crore electors)

1. **National VTR 66.40%**; 55.38 crore votes, 28.03 crore non-voters.
2. **Women voted less than men**: female VTR 65.63% vs male 67.09% (gap +1.46 pp). Yet in **165 of 543 PCs women out-voted men**.
3. **Regional split is the story**: North-East 79.5%, South 72.5%, East 69.3%, Central 63.7%, West 61.6%, North 61.3%. Central and West carry the biggest gender gaps (7.6 pp and 5.4 pp); the East is the only region where women out-vote men (-1.46 pp).
4. **Madhya Pradesh is the gender-gap hotspot** (9.5 pp state gap). Its seats Rajgarh, Damoh, Morena, Bhind have male leads of 16-18 pp. Bihar (Jhanjharpur, Madhubani) and Tamil Nadu (Sivaganga, Ramanathapuram) show the opposite: women ahead by 10-13 pp.
5. **Mobilisation potential is concentrated**: Uttar Pradesh (58.35%) alone would add ~1.12 crore votes at the national VTR; Bihar ~64 lakh; Maharashtra ~49 lakh. Together about 2.25 crore of the ~2.97 crore total shortfall among below-average states.
6. **Low turnout is a big-city / big-electorate pattern**: constituency size correlates negatively with turnout (r = -0.45). Biggest constituency, Malkajgiri (31.8 lakh electors), polled 50.9%; Kalyan 42.9%, Patna Sahib 45.3%, Bangalore North 56.5%.
7. **Jammu & Kashmir is the extreme**: state VTR 49.5%, and a 21 pp std dev across its 6 PCs (Srinagar 25.9% to Ladakh 70.8%).
8. **Spread**: PC std dev 10.3 pp (CV 15%); 27.8% of PCs below 60%, 1.8% below 50%, 26.9% at 75% or above.
9. Gender gap and turnout are weakly negatively correlated (r = -0.21): where turnout is low, men tend to lead more.
